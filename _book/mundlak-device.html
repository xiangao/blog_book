8.1 Introduction: Fixed Effects vs. Mundlak Regression

In panel data models with individual effects \(c_i\):

\[y_{it} = X_{it}\beta + c_i + u_{it}\]

Standard Fixed Effects (within estimation) eliminates \(c_i\) by demeaning, allowing \(c_i\) to correlate arbitrarily with \(X_{it}\). However, within estimation cannot estimate the effects of time-invariant variables (e.g., gender, race, schooling) because their within-unit variance is zero.

Mundlak (1978) proposed modeling \(c_i\) as a linear projection on the unit-specific means of the time-varying covariates:

\[c_i = \bar{X}_i \theta + \eta_i, \qquad E[\eta_i \mid X_i] = 0\]

Substituting into the regression yields:

\[y_{it} = X_{it}\beta + \bar{X}_i \theta + \eta_i + u_{it}\]

This equation can be estimated by Pooled OLS (POLS) or Random Effects (RE). By the Frisch–Waugh–Lovell (FWL) theorem, the resulting estimate of \(\beta\) is algebraically identical to the fixed-effects estimator.

Furthermore: 1. Hausman Test: Testing \(H_0: \theta = 0\) is asymptotically equivalent to the classical Hausman test, and works directly with cluster-robust standard errors. 2. Time-Invariant Regressors: Time-invariant covariates can be included directly in the regression. Because within-unit deviations are orthogonal to any time-invariant regressor by construction, their inclusion does not alter \(\hat{\beta}\).

8.3 Two-Way Mundlak (TWM) and the Balanced Panel Condition

Wooldridge (2021) extends Mundlak’s idea to Two-Way Fixed Effects (TWFE):

\[y_{it} = X_{it}\beta + c_i + f_t + u_{it}\]

The Two-Way Mundlak (TWM) estimator adds both unit means (\(\bar{X}_{i\cdot}\)) and time means (\(\bar{X}_{\cdot t}\)):

\[y_{it} = X_{it}\beta + \bar{X}_{i\cdot}\lambda + \bar{X}_{\cdot t}\theta + u_{it}\]

8.3.1 The Unbalanced Panel Trap

Equivalence between TWM and TWFE requires the unit and time factors to be orthogonal, which holds automatically in balanced panels where every unit is observed in every period.

In unbalanced panels, simple unit and time means do not span the space of two-way dummies. Equivalence is restored by including the unit means of the time dummies (\(\bar{d}_{it}\)), which conditions on each unit’s observation pattern across time (\(s_{it}/T_i\)).

Code
preserve
webuse nlswork, clear
keep if !missing(ln_wage, age, union)
xtset idcode year

bysort idcode: egen m_age   = mean(age)
bysort idcode: egen m_union = mean(union)
egen t_age   = mean(age),   by(year)
egen t_union = mean(union), by(year)

* Unit means of time dummies
levelsof year, local(yrs)
foreach y of local yrs {
    bysort idcode: egen my_`y' = mean(year==`y')
}

quietly xtreg ln_wage age union i.year, fe
scalar b_twfe = _b[age]

* Naive TWM with unit and time means only
quietly regress ln_wage age union m_age m_union t_age t_union
scalar b_twm = _b[age]

* Correct TWM with unit means of time dummies
quietly regress ln_wage age union m_age m_union i.year my_*
scalar b_fix = _b[age]

8.4 Multi-Way Fixed Effects (\(>2\) Dimensions)

With three or more fixed-effect factors (e.g., individual \(i\), year \(t\), and group \(g\)): - Do not compute one set of means per dimension unless factors are mutually orthogonal (e.g., a Latin square experiment). - General Rule: Select the high-dimensional factor to eliminate (typically individual \(i\)), retain dummy indicators for the remaining lower-dimensional factors, and project onto the within-\(i\) means of all time-varying regressors, including the retained dummies.

Code
preserve
capture program drop twm3
program define twm3
    args label
    quietly {
        capture drop x_i x_t x_g mt_* mg_* x y ft hg
        sort t
        by t: gen double ft = rnormal() if _n==1
        by t: replace ft = ft[1]
        sort g
        by g: gen double hg = rnormal() if _n==1
        by g: replace hg = hg[1]
        gen double x = 0.6*ci + 0.5*ft + 0.4*hg + rnormal()
        gen double y = 1*x + ci + ft + hg + rnormal()
        bysort i: egen double x_i = mean(x)
        bysort t: egen double x_t = mean(x)
        bysort g: egen double x_g = mean(x)
        levelsof t, local(ts)
        foreach k of local ts {
            bysort i: egen double mt_`k' = mean(t==`k')
        }
        levelsof g, local(gs)
        foreach k of local gs {
            bysort i: egen double mg_`k' = mean(g==`k')
        }
        areg y x i.t i.g, absorb(i)
        scalar b_fe = _b[x]
        regress y x x_i x_t x_g
        scalar b_A = _b[x]
        regress y x x_i i.t i.g mt_* mg_*
        scalar b_B = _b[x]
    }
    di "`label'"
    di "   Three-way FE                      = " %9.6f b_fe
    di "   A: One set of means per factor     = " %9.6f b_A  "   gap: " %9.6f (b_A-b_fe)
    di "   B: Means over i, dummies included  = " %9.6f b_B  "   gap: " %9.6f (b_B-b_fe)
end

8.5 Application to Difference-in-Differences (Common Timing)

In standard DiD with common treatment timing: \[y_{it} = \beta w_{it} + c_i + f_t + u_{it}\] where \(w_{it} = d_i \cdot \text{post}_t\). Because \(d_i\) is time-invariant and \(\text{post}_t\) is unit-invariant: - Unit mean: \(\bar{w}_{i\cdot} = d_i \cdot \bar{p}\) - Time mean: \(\bar{w}_{\cdot t} = \bar{d} \cdot \text{post}_t\)

Therefore, the Mundlak projection is spanned simply by including \(d_i\) and the post-treatment dummy (or full time dummies). This framework makes it straightforward to model heterogeneous treatment effects over time:

Code

gen w2 = rnormal()

gen w=d*post

gen u = rnormal()

gen f2014=(year==2014)
gen f2015=(year==2015)

* DGP with growing treatment effect: 2.5 in 2014, 3.5 in 2015 (ATT = 3.0)

8.6 Summary

  1. One-Way Mundlak: Adding unit averages \(\bar{X}_i\) to Pooled OLS or Random Effects recovers the within (FE) estimator and enables estimation of time-invariant variables.
  2. Two-Way Mundlak: Exact equivalence with TWFE holds unconditionally in balanced panels. In unbalanced panels, one must also include unit means of time dummies.
  3. Multi-Way FE: When more than two fixed effects are present, eliminate the high-dimensional factor with means and retain explicit dummy indicators for the lower-dimensional factors.
  4. Difference-in-Differences: Mundlak formulation allows flexible event-study interactions and time-invariant moderators within a transparent OLS/GLS framework.

## Introduction: Fixed Effects vs. Mundlak Regression

In panel data models with individual effects $c_i$:

$$y_{it} = X_{it}\beta + c_i + u_{it}$$

Standard Fixed Effects (within estimation) eliminates $c_i$ by demeaning, allowing $c_i$ to correlate arbitrarily with $X_{it}$. However, within estimation cannot estimate the effects of time-invariant variables (e.g., gender, race, schooling) because their within-unit variance is zero.

Mundlak (1978) proposed modeling $c_i$ as a linear projection on the unit-specific means of the time-varying covariates:

$$c_i = \bar{X}_i \theta + \eta_i, \qquad E[\eta_i \mid X_i] = 0$$

Substituting into the regression yields:

$$y_{it} = X_{it}\beta + \bar{X}_i \theta + \eta_i + u_{it}$$

This equation can be estimated by Pooled OLS (POLS) or Random Effects (RE). By the Frisch–Waugh–Lovell (FWL) theorem, the resulting estimate of $\beta$ is algebraically identical to the fixed-effects estimator.

Furthermore:
1. **Hausman Test:** Testing $H_0: \theta = 0$ is asymptotically equivalent to the classical Hausman test, and works directly with cluster-robust standard errors.
2. **Time-Invariant Regressors:** Time-invariant covariates can be included directly in the regression. Because within-unit deviations are orthogonal to any time-invariant regressor by construction, their inclusion does not alter $\hat{\beta}$.

```{stata}
xtreg ln_wage age union mean_age mean_union, re cluster(idcode)
```

All three models yield identical coefficients: $\hat{\beta}_{\text{age}} = 0.0153507$ ($\text{SE} = 0.00069$) and $\hat{\beta}_{\text{union}} = 0.1055274$ ($\text{SE} = 0.00986$).

### Estimating Time-Invariant Covariates

We add `race` (constant within individual) to the Mundlak Pooled OLS model:

```{stata}
*| label: stata2
*| echo: true
*| collectcode: true

reg ln_wage age union mean_age mean_union i.race, cluster(idcode)
```

The coefficients on `age` and `union` remain unchanged to seven digits, while the coefficient on `race` is identified (Black women earn $-17.9\%$ relative to white women, $\text{SE} = 0.0144$).

## Two-Way Mundlak (TWM) and the Balanced Panel Condition

Wooldridge (2021) extends Mundlak's idea to Two-Way Fixed Effects (TWFE):

$$y_{it} = X_{it}\beta + c_i + f_t + u_{it}$$

The Two-Way Mundlak (TWM) estimator adds both unit means ($\bar{X}_{i\cdot}$) and time means ($\bar{X}_{\cdot t}$):

$$y_{it} = X_{it}\beta + \bar{X}_{i\cdot}\lambda + \bar{X}_{\cdot t}\theta + u_{it}$$

### The Unbalanced Panel Trap

Equivalence between TWM and TWFE requires the unit and time factors to be **orthogonal**, which holds automatically in **balanced panels** where every unit is observed in every period.

In **unbalanced panels**, simple unit and time means do not span the space of two-way dummies. Equivalence is restored by including the **unit means of the time dummies** ($\bar{d}_{it}$), which conditions on each unit's observation pattern across time ($s_{it}/T_i$).

```{stata}
*| label: twm_unbalanced
*| echo: true
*| collectcode: true

preserve
webuse nlswork, clear
keep if !missing(ln_wage, age, union)
xtset idcode year

bysort idcode: egen m_age   = mean(age)
bysort idcode: egen m_union = mean(union)
egen t_age   = mean(age),   by(year)
egen t_union = mean(union), by(year)

* Unit means of time dummies
levelsof year, local(yrs)
foreach y of local yrs {
    bysort idcode: egen my_`y' = mean(year==`y')
}

quietly xtreg ln_wage age union i.year, fe
scalar b_twfe = _b[age]

* Naive TWM with unit and time means only
quietly regress ln_wage age union m_age m_union t_age t_union
scalar b_twm = _b[age]

* Correct TWM with unit means of time dummies
quietly regress ln_wage age union m_age m_union i.year my_*
scalar b_fix = _b[age]

di "TWFE                           age = " %9.7f b_twfe
di "Naive TWM (unit + time means)  age = " %9.7f b_twm
di "TWM + unit means of dummies    age = " %9.7f b_fix

restore
```

On this unbalanced panel:
- TWFE gives $\hat{\beta}_{\text{age}} = 0.0276645$.
- Naive TWM gives $0.0117968$ (severely biased, off by a factor of $2.35\times$).
- Adding the unit means of the time dummies restores exact numerical equivalence to 6 decimal places ($0.0276646$).

## Multi-Way Fixed Effects ($>2$ Dimensions)

With three or more fixed-effect factors (e.g., individual $i$, year $t$, and group $g$):
- Do **not** compute one set of means per dimension unless factors are mutually orthogonal (e.g., a Latin square experiment).
- **General Rule:** Select the high-dimensional factor to eliminate (typically individual $i$), retain dummy indicators for the remaining lower-dimensional factors, and project onto the within-$i$ means of all time-varying regressors, including the retained dummies.

```{stata}
*| label: twm_three_way
*| echo: true
*| collectcode: true

preserve
capture program drop twm3
program define twm3
    args label
    quietly {
        capture drop x_i x_t x_g mt_* mg_* x y ft hg
        sort t
        by t: gen double ft = rnormal() if _n==1
        by t: replace ft = ft[1]
        sort g
        by g: gen double hg = rnormal() if _n==1
        by g: replace hg = hg[1]
        gen double x = 0.6*ci + 0.5*ft + 0.4*hg + rnormal()
        gen double y = 1*x + ci + ft + hg + rnormal()
        bysort i: egen double x_i = mean(x)
        bysort t: egen double x_t = mean(x)
        bysort g: egen double x_g = mean(x)
        levelsof t, local(ts)
        foreach k of local ts {
            bysort i: egen double mt_`k' = mean(t==`k')
        }
        levelsof g, local(gs)
        foreach k of local gs {
            bysort i: egen double mg_`k' = mean(g==`k')
        }
        areg y x i.t i.g, absorb(i)
        scalar b_fe = _b[x]
        regress y x x_i x_t x_g
        scalar b_A = _b[x]
        regress y x x_i i.t i.g mt_* mg_*
        scalar b_B = _b[x]
    }
    di "`label'"
    di "   Three-way FE                      = " %9.6f b_fe
    di "   A: One set of means per factor     = " %9.6f b_A  "   gap: " %9.6f (b_A-b_fe)
    di "   B: Means over i, dummies included  = " %9.6f b_B  "   gap: " %9.6f (b_B-b_fe)
end

clear
set seed 20260731
set obs 300
gen i = _n
gen double ci = rnormal()
expand 10
bysort i: gen t = _n

* Non-orthogonal design (random assignment of g)
gen g = 1 + int(runiform()*5)
twm3 "Non-orthogonal factor assignment:"

* Orthogonal design (Latin square)
replace g = mod(i+t,5)+1
twm3 "Orthogonal factor assignment (Latin square):"

capture program drop twm3
restore
```

Construction B (means over $i$ including factor dummies) is exact in both orthogonal and non-orthogonal designs.

## Application to Difference-in-Differences (Common Timing)

In standard DiD with common treatment timing:
$$y_{it} = \beta w_{it} + c_i + f_t + u_{it}$$
where $w_{it} = d_i \cdot \text{post}_t$. Because $d_i$ is time-invariant and $\text{post}_t$ is unit-invariant:
- Unit mean: $\bar{w}_{i\cdot} = d_i \cdot \bar{p}$
- Time mean: $\bar{w}_{\cdot t} = \bar{d} \cdot \text{post}_t$

Therefore, the Mundlak projection is spanned simply by including $d_i$ and the post-treatment dummy (or full time dummies). This framework makes it straightforward to model heterogeneous treatment effects over time:

```{stata}
*| label: stata3
*| echo: true
*| collectcode: true

clear
set obs 100
set seed 1234567
gen id = _n
gen w1 = rnormal()
gen w2 = rnormal()
expand 4
bysort id: gen year=2011+_n
gen post=(year>2013)
gen d=(id>50)
gen w=d*post
gen x1 = rnormal() + w1 + 2*w2
gen u = rnormal()

gen f2014=(year==2014)
gen f2015=(year==2015)

* DGP with growing treatment effect: 2.5 in 2014, 3.5 in 2015 (ATT = 3.0)
gen y1 = 1 + 2*x1 + 3*w1 + u
gen logy = y1 + 2.5*d*f2014 + 3.5*d*f2015

xtset id year
```

```{stata}
*| label: stata4
*| echo: true
*| collectcode: true

* 1. TWFE with year-specific treatment effects
xtreg logy c.w#c.f2014 c.w#c.f2015 i.year, fe vce(cluster id)

* 2. Pooled OLS with Mundlak controls (d and year dummies)
reg logy c.w#c.f2014 c.w#c.f2015 d f2014 f2015, vce(cluster id)
lincom (c.w#c.f2014 + c.w#c.f2015)/2

* 3. Adding time-invariant controls (x1 and interaction with d)
reg logy c.w#c.f2014 c.w#c.f2015 d f2014 f2015 x1 c.d#c.x1, vce(cluster id)
```

The POLS Mundlak model reproduces the exact TWFE estimates for each post-treatment year (2.5 and 3.5), yields the average treatment effect of 3.0 via `lincom`, and allows time-invariant controls and group interactions without disturbing the treatment effect estimates.

## Summary

1. **One-Way Mundlak:** Adding unit averages $\bar{X}_i$ to Pooled OLS or Random Effects recovers the within (FE) estimator and enables estimation of time-invariant variables.
2. **Two-Way Mundlak:** Exact equivalence with TWFE holds unconditionally in balanced panels. In unbalanced panels, one must also include unit means of time dummies.
3. **Multi-Way FE:** When more than two fixed effects are present, eliminate the high-dimensional factor with means and retain explicit dummy indicators for the lower-dimensional factors.
4. **Difference-in-Differences:** Mundlak formulation allows flexible event-study interactions and time-invariant moderators within a transparent OLS/GLS framework.
